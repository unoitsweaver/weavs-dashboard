CDM

The Principal Designer duty, explained without the jargon.

If you are having construction or refurbishment work done, the CDM Regulations place legal duties on you and on the people you appoint. Here is what those duties actually mean, and where we can help.

What CDM is

The Construction (Design and Management) Regulations 2015 apply to almost all construction work in Great Britain, including many refurbishments, extensions and demolition jobs, not only large new-build sites.

The regulations exist so that health and safety is planned from the start of a project, not bolted on once work has begun. They set out duties for the client, for designers, for contractors, and — on projects with more than one contractor — for a Principal Designer and a Principal Contractor.

Who does what

The client

The organisation or person who has the work done. You must make sure the right duty-holders are appointed, that those people have the skills and resources for the job, and that information is passed on. On a commercial project this duty cannot simply be handed away.

The Principal Designer

Plans, manages, monitors and coordinates health and safety in the pre-construction phase: while the project is being designed and prepared. They help the client, work with designers, and see that pre-construction information and the health and safety file are properly handled.

The Principal Contractor

Plans, manages, monitors and coordinates health and safety in the construction phase, including the construction phase plan and the running of the site.

Documents that actually help

Three pieces of information sit at the centre of CDM, and they are only useful if people can follow them:

  • Pre-construction information — what designers and contractors need to know before they start, including residual risks and existing records.
  • The construction phase plan — how the Principal Contractor will manage the work safely on site.
  • The health and safety file — information you will need for future maintenance, alteration or demolition, handed over at the end.

If you are unsure which duty sits with you, or who you should appoint, get in touch. We can talk it through in plain English and agree a sensible way forward for your project.
